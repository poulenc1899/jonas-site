Can you screen nearly 6,000 papers for a systematic review in two days? We tried — with a "crowd of oracles" and [ASReview](https://asreview.nl).

Systematic reviews are essential for evidence-based research, but screening is slow and hard to scale. Human-only screening can be inconsistent, while fully automated approaches using large language models often lack the contextual judgement complex decisions need. In this paper we introduce a **crowd-based screening method that pairs human expertise with adaptive machine learning**.

### Key takeaways

- **The crowd of oracles.** 27 experts from the IHI IMPROVE consortium worked in parallel on a single, shared active-learning model, screening 5,842 papers across 11 disease topics related to patient-generated health data in two days.
- **Post-processing matters.** To protect the quality of the 487 inclusions we used topic reallocation, re-screening with a 50-record stopping rule, targeted full-text checks, noisy-label filtering and a re-evaluation label to catch missed studies.
- **Collaboration works.** Engagement peaked during informal, shared screening sessions (even around a pool!), and surveys showed increased trust in AI-assisted reviewing afterwards.

We later turned this work into a workshop at the HTAi 2026 annual meeting in Istanbul — see *Talks & outreach*.
