**English title:** *Staying Positive: Psychosocial and Socioeconomic Determinants of Psychiatric Morbidity in Dutch Young Adults Living with HIV.*

Despite widespread access to HIV prevention and care, a second epidemic is emerging in the Netherlands: a mental health crisis among young people living with HIV. This paper, based on my BSc thesis at the University of Amsterdam, asks how socioeconomic status (SES) shapes mental health in this group, and whether stigma and health literacy explain that relationship.

### What we did

We collected new survey data in the Randstad (N = 61) using validated instruments (PHQ-8, PAN-Stigma, IMTA), and combined regression, structural equation modelling and **symptom network analysis**.

### What we found

- 86% of participants reported moderate or more severe depressive symptoms — despite excellent HIV care.
- Participants from lower socioeconomic backgrounds showed **denser, more tightly connected** depressive symptom networks, with suicidal thoughts far more central.
- SES predicted internalised HIV stigma, and low health literacy was linked to denser symptom structures.

SES seems to shape *how* depression is experienced more than *how severe* it is. A standard screening score can't tell you whether someone's symptoms are loosely scattered or locked into a self-reinforcing cluster — and that difference may determine who is most at risk of crisis or of disengaging from care.
